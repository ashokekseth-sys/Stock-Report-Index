Company Overview

What They Do

Rolex Rings makes forged and machined steel rings and components: bearing rings (the races inside ball and roller bearings) and auto components, sold to bearing and automotive customers in India and abroad (17+ countries per the Aug 2026 investor presentation). It is listed on the NSE and reports a single segment; the product split below is from a call summary (FY26 about 47% bearing rings, 45% auto components, 8% other). 57 customers are named in the Aug 2026 presentation; a secondary TradingView post puts the top-10 at about 80% of revenue (low confidence). Customers are named only in a broker note (section 02, US-business block), not by management; the top-5 groups are 65–70% of business per the Q4 FY26 call (18 May 2026), while the top-10 share was not found in any primary document. Forging-technology detail, wind and EV detail were not found.

Reported figures: FY26 sales ₹1,143.50 Cr (MarketSmith) / ₹1,144 Cr (stockscans), PAT about ₹141 Cr; TTM sales ₹1,274 Cr; TTM EPS ₹6.73 (my arithmetic: 1.75 − 0.01 + 2.21 + 2.78). Basis warning: MarketSmith's annual EPS (FY26 51.81) is on the pre-split share basis and its quarterly EPS (~₹2) on the post-split basis; the two must not be mixed (a 1:10 split, FV ₹10 to Re 1, board-approved 4 Sep 2025; record date conflicting: 19 Sep, 4 Oct or 17 Oct 2025). The balance sheet has no borrowings and ₹72.9 Cr of cash (Sep-26); a ₹180 Cr buyback was completed (timing and price not found).

Key Competitive Advantage: a debt-free, cash-generative forging business with EBIT margins of 20–21% for five years (MarketSmith) and a growing export and auto-component mix. The flip side: bearing rings were flat in H1 FY27, capacity utilisation is only about 63–65%, a very large part of revenue sits with a few customers (low-confidence source), and the latest quarter carries a ~₹18 Cr refund inside revenue and EBITDA (section 03).

US Business — What the Documents Say (added v2)

US share of revenue: three figures, not like-for-like. (a) ~25% of revenue from the USA, ~20-odd% Europe, a couple of % Canada / Mexico (CFO, Q1 FY26 call, 12 Aug 2025; summary, unverified). (b) “One-third” (CFO, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 2026) is one-third of export bearing-ring business only, not of total revenue. (c) Q2 FY27 US component revenue ~₹92 Cr, more than double the year before (summary, unverified); share of total not stated. My arithmetic: 92 / 389 = ~24% of total revenue (about 28% of the ₹330 Cr component sales); ₹389 Cr is the same unverified summary. Dalal & Broacha (5 Mar 2026) gives no US figure; it says Europe is ~65% of new-order intake.

Tariffs, as documented. Q4 FY26 call (18 May 2026): ₹22 Cr of US customs duty paid, about half still receivable from customers; the 25% Section 232 duty since Nov 2025 is reimbursed by customers; IEEPA duty paid up to 31 Oct 2025 was expected to be refunded after the Supreme Court ruling and booked in revenue (expected ~Sep 2026), part to be passed back to customers; the 2.97% base duty is not refunded. US business fell ~10% QoQ in Q3 FY26 and was “almost 30% down” annualised vs FY25 (CFO, Q3 FY26). Q1 FY27 (6 Aug 2026): a couple of US orders / programmes on hold at the OEM, the rest returning gradually.

Recovery. Largest-customer volume fell 35–40% in FY26 and is back to the FY25 level; a couple of US programmes remain on hold; no customer lost (Q2 FY27 summary, unverified). Q2 FY27 also shows ~₹15 Cr of customer reimbursement received and ~₹10 Cr of duty paid but unrecovered (same summary).

Customs-duty refund: ~₹18 Cr vs ~₹21 Cr. Both figures appear in the same Q2 FY27 call summary (5 Nov 2026), the second in answer to an analyst prompt; booked as one-time revenue. Not resolved without the raw transcript, so the ~₹18 Cr used elsewhere in this report is one of two figures.

Who names which customers. Broker note only: Dalal & Broacha (5 Mar 2026, analyst Animesh Jain) cites “long-standing relationships with global bearing leaders such as Timken, SKF and Schaeffler” and ~75% of the portfolio on an exclusive basis. Management named none in the calls read; on the Q1 FY27 call an analyst asked about a Timken recovery and the CFO said there was “not much change” with that customer. Allison, GM and Magna were raised by analysts, not by management. Management did not confirm the Timken / SKF / Schaeffler names or the 75% exclusivity.

Conflicts Kept, Both Numbers Shown

Capacity: 165,000 MTPA (Dalal & Broacha, 5 Mar 2026; also “75 million parts a year”) vs 105,000–115,000 MTPA achievable (CFO, Q3 FY26 call). Customer concentration: top-5 groups 65–70% (CFO, Q4 FY26 call, across 15+ plants / customers) vs top-10 ~80% (earlier TradingView post, low confidence). The two are not contradictory on their face, but the top-10 share was not found in any primary document. Balance sheet: net cash ₹367 Cr (a claim in the user-supplied Grok summary; no primary document found) vs ₹72.9 Cr cash at Sep-26 (MarketSmith read); a Q2 FY27 summary also mentions ~₹260 Cr of cash, bank and liquid investments. The definition is open: cash only vs cash plus investments, and whether net of anything.

What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

An approval-based forging position does not protect against a tariff change, a freight-cost spike (ocean freight up nearly 3x after the West Asia escalation), a labour shortage (it hit Q1 FY27), a customer re-sourcing, or a weak bearing cycle. It also does not make a one-off customs refund recur.

Synthesis: a narrow, process-and-approval moat with an unusually clean balance sheet. Returns have fallen from the FY22–23 peak (ROCE 33.17% and 31.29% to 19.18% in FY26; ROE 24.20% / 26.66% to 11.62%) because sales have been flat for four years (FY23 ₹1,178.95 Cr to FY26 ₹1,143.50 Cr) on a larger equity base. The H1 FY27 acceleration is the first real test of whether that is changing.
