Company Overview

What They Do

Adani Ports is India's largest private port operator, handling 27.1% of all the cargo moved through Indian ports and 45.5% of its containers. FY26 was the first year any Indian operator passed 500 MMT (500.8 MMT, +11%). The network runs 16 Indian ports and terminals on both coasts — Mundra, Krishnapatnam, Dhamra, Hazira, Gangavaram, Vizhinjam and others — totalling 671 MTPA of capacity after the Paradip win announced on the date of this report.

It is not only a port company. Four segments sit alongside: logistics (rail, trucking, warehousing, 132 rakes), marine (tugs and dredging), SEZ and port development on a 16,000+ hectare land bank, and since FY26 a material international arm — Haifa, Colombo, Dar es Salaam and NQXT Australia.

Key Competitive Advantage: irreplaceable deep-draft sites held on long concessions with the right to set its own tariffs, sitting on a land bank that manufactures its own captive cargo. Mundra alone moved 192 MMT — more than any of India's twelve government major ports.

Competitive Advantage (Moat) Analysis

DimensionRatingCommentary
Brand StrengthWeak / N.A.Shipping lines choose on draft, connectivity, turnaround and cost — not brand, and APSEZ's own disclosures list exactly those. Where the name does matter it has been a cost: Norway's Government Pension Fund Global excluded APSEZ in May 2024, and MSCI only lifted its ESG rating from CCC to B in FY26. MSC and CMA CGM took 50% equity in the Mundra terminals rather than simply buying berth services.
Network EffectsModerateThe physical network is real — both coasts, 671 MTPA, 12 multi-modal parks, 132 rakes — but the compounding is asserted more than demonstrated. Logistics EBITDA margin fell to 19.3% from 22.3%, rail container volume has declined every month this year (−19%, −22%, −16%), and logistics earns the group's worst RoCE at 10%. Non-port segments are only ~10% of EBITDA. Adding a port makes APSEZ more valuable to itself; that it makes each port more valuable to a given customer is not yet visible in the numbers.
Switching CostsModerateGenuine but asset-specific, not franchise-wide. 53% of domestic cargo is “sticky” (down from 56%), NQXT has 40 of 50 MMT under take-or-pay to 2110, and shipping lines with terminal equity cannot cheaply relocate. The other 47% is not sticky, and it showed: Mundra's volume fell 4% while non-Mundra ports grew 13%. Bulk cargo moved when the economics changed.
Cost AdvantageModerateReal at the revenue line, absent at the return line. EBITDA per tonne is ₹456 against JSW Infrastructure's ₹214 — but ROCE FY26 is 10.9% vs JSW's 11.2%, and on a like-for-like ROIC basis APSEZ (9.9%), JSW (10.3%) and CONCOR (9.8%) are indistinguishable. The gap comes from owning the quays, the dredgers and the land rather than renting them — so what peers pay as concession fee sits in Adani's depreciation and capital base instead. Employee cost is 6.1% of revenue against PSA's 22.9%, which is the largest single hard driver.
Concessions & Regulatory PositionStrong — and this is the moatCARE states APSEZ has pricing flexibility at all domestic locations except three terminals. TAMP's tariff mandate was withdrawn in 2021; non-major ports set their own prices. Against that stands a 16,000-hectare land bank (~12,500 ha at Mundra) that generates its own captive cargo, and newest wins are 30-year concessions. A competing deep-water entrant faces what Vizhinjam faced: over a decade and central viability-gap funding.
Where the 73% Margin Actually Comes From

The most useful cross-check available sits inside APSEZ's own accounts. Its container joint ventures at Mundra — same land, same country, same management, same accounting standard — earn:

Those JVs run below ICTSI's 66%. So the 73% is not superior container-terminal operations — it is non-container bulk, land economics, and captive marine work. Note the “Harbour” dredging line: ₹3,805 Cr EBITDA on ₹4,278 Cr revenue, an 89% margin, roughly a fifth of domestic-ports EBITDA. Vizhinjam's 86% likewise includes ₹316 Cr of Kerala government O&M support booked in both revenue and EBITDA.

Synthesis: the moat is the concession-plus-land position — deep-draft sites nobody else can assemble, on long tenures, with the right to set tariffs, feeding off a land bank that creates its own cargo. Nothing else survives scrutiny: brand is irrelevant, the network is asserted, switching costs cover about half the book, and the cost advantage evaporates at the ROCE line.
What it does not protect against — the grantor. Every element of that moat is a state grant, and the most valuable one expires on 16 February 2031. As of today APSEZ holds only a non-binding Letter of Comfort from the Gujarat Maritime Board (Aug 2026); tenure and tariff-sharing are unsettled, and it asked for 45 further years against a 50-year policy ceiling. The FY26 accounts also disclose that the CT-3, CT-3 Extension and CT-4 sub-concessions await Government of Gujarat approval for final signing, and the West Basin supplementary concession is still “in the process of getting signed” — despite that terminal operating since February 2011. Management flagged the renewal as a risk on the Q1 call, and the company's own risk register ranks Political (R1) and Regulatory (R2) above every commercial risk.

What Happened & Why It Matters
A dated, verifiable de-risking chain. The overhang that has sat on this stock since November 2024 came off in three steps. 14 May 2026: the SEC matter settled by consent — $6m from Gautam Adani and $12m from Sagar Adani, without admitting or denying, and critically no disgorgement and no officer-and-director bars, which would have forced them off the boards. 25 June: S&P upgraded APSEZ to BBB, matching India's sovereign rating, citing a tightened leverage policy; every Hindenburg-era negative outlook has now been reversed and JCR rates it a notch above the sovereign. 10 August: Judge Garaufis dismissed the securities- and wire-fraud counts against Gautam Adani, Sagar Adani and Vneet Jaain with prejudice — they cannot be refiled. Eighteen million dollars against a ₹4 lakh crore market capitalisation is a very light landing, and the forward multiple re-rated from 22.5x to 26.9x across exactly this window.
But this was not a vindication, and the presiding judge said so. In a 47-page ruling Garaufis wrote that “the irregularities in the decision to dismiss the indictment are concerning”, called it “highly unusual” that the decision was taken “largely in collaboration with defense counsel… seemingly without input” from the investigating agencies, and rejected DOJ's claim that India had investigated the bribery — finding the documents showed a decision not to. He refused to dismiss FCPA and obstruction counts against the co-defendants, and denied DOJ's renewed bid on 5 September — four days before this report. The case against the principals ended on a government motion the court openly criticised, not on the merits.

That is exoneration on timing, not on substance, and the distinction belongs in any honest reading of the governance record.

Overall Sentiment & Momentum Prediction

Key Watch-Item
⚠ The Moat Has an Expiry Date

Mundra's concession — 192 MMT, larger than any Indian government port, on a 12,500-hectare land bank — expires 16 February 2031, and APSEZ holds only a non-binding Letter of Comfort. The CT-3, CT-3 Extension and CT-4 sub-concessions still await Government of Gujarat approval for final signing. Immediate catalyst: the cabotage decision deferred to October 2026, which governs whether foreign-flagged vessels may run domestic coastal legs — the economics Vizhinjam's entire transhipment model rests on.

Framing note: this is an infrastructure annuity being bought as a duration asset, not a cyclical bet on Indian trade — and the distinction matters, because the trade data is weak. All-India cargo grew 4.4% against GDP of 7.8%, so ports are compounding at just 0.6x GDP; APSEZ's own domestic volume grew 5%. With a five-year beta of 0.49, the stock rose 3.8% on a day the Sensex fell 1.08% and has outperformed a falling index by roughly 40 points over twelve months. What the market is paying for is the concession annuity plus a capex-funded growth option — which is precisely why the 2031 expiry, not the next quarter's volumes, is the variable that matters.

Key Ratios — Industry-Contextualized

EV / EBITDA vs Global Port Operators
APSEZ is the most expensive listed port operator in the world — and simultaneously cheaper than its Indian peer. That is the whole valuation debate in one chart.
Why EV/EBITDA and not P/E. On P/E, APSEZ (30.8x) looks cheaper than JSW Infrastructure (48.2x) and dearer than Shipping Corporation (8.3x) — both artefacts of leverage and depreciation policy rather than business quality. On EV/EBITDA the picture is legible: the dispersion across seven listed ports-and-logistics names runs 6.1x to 50.4x, because these assets are priced on concession life and growth runway, not on current margin.
The template for what a running-down concession does to a multiple is already listed. Gujarat Pipavav trades at 8.9x with the best returns in the peer set — ROCE 28.1%, ROIC 27.7% — and a 6.3% dividend yield, on a mature single-port asset. The market is amortising its finite concession. That is precisely the mechanism to hold in mind for Mundra in 2031.

The auditor record — the most substantive governance finding on APSEZ itself. Deloitte resigned on 12 August 2023, one year into a fresh five-year term, citing “a lack of a wider audit role” across the group; the Audit Committee publicly recorded that the grounds were “not convincing or sufficient.” Its FY23 report was a qualified opinion — standalone, consolidated and internal financial controls — over ₹3,750 Cr consolidated recoverable from an EPC contractor linked to a party named in the short-seller report. Deloitte's objection was that the company's own evaluation “does not constitute sufficient appropriate audit evidence.” The qualification survived the auditor change: successor MSKA qualified FY24 as well, finding that “the Company does not have an internal control system for identifying and confirming related party relationships.” Two consecutive years, two different firms, the same material weakness. FY25 and FY26 are clean — lifted only after management commissioned the independent review Deloitte had said was missing.
Two items survive the clean FY26 opinion. The auditor could not confirm the audit trail was enabled for direct database changes between 27 May and 12 Dec 2025 — a 6.5-month gap — with the company noting logs “purged due to technical constraints”. And audit-scope concentration has worsened sharply, which is precisely what Deloitte complained about: subsidiaries audited by other firms rose from 85 (FY23) to 184 (FY26), holding ₹1.2 lakh Cr of assets and 30% of consolidated revenue, on which the principal auditor relies “solely on the reports of the other auditors”. The internal auditor has changed twice in twelve months, and the CFO changed in Feb 2026 with no reason disclosed.
The NQXT Round Trip

APSEZ bought the Abbot Point terminal from the Queensland Government in FY12 at an EV of A$1.829bn, then divested it in FY13 at A$2.051bn “to deleverage”. In December 2025 it bought the same asset back from the promoter family — Carmichael Rail and Port Singapore Holdings, which the company's own deck states plainly “is a related party” — at an EV of A$3,975m, roughly double its own exit price, settled in 14.38 Cr newly issued shares rather than cash. Promoter holding rose 2.13% to 68.02%; minorities were diluted 6.7%.

Sector Rotation

Ports sit awkwardly on a rotation wheel, and the honest answer is that they occupy two positions at once: early-cycle on volumes, because trade turns before domestic demand, and late-cycle on capital deployment, because capacity is added at the top and depreciates through the trough. India is in a mid-cycle expansion — GDP 7.8% in the June quarter, manufacturing +9.2% — which is the composition that generates port volumes.

That is the honest reason no single multiple values it, and why the report leans on EV/EBITDA and the concession clock rather than a peer P/E.

Risk Analysis

Risk CategoryEvidenceSeverity
Economic & trade cyclicalityVolumes are an index of Indian EXIM, and the trade side is the weak link: July 2026 exports $44.2bn against imports $76.2bn, a $32.0bn deficit and widening, with US tariffs biting textiles, shrimp and gems. All-India cargo grew 4.4% against GDP of 7.8% — ports are compounding at just 0.6x GDP, well below the 1.0–1.2x most models assume. FY26 showed it directly: Mundra −4%, EXIM coal −7.1 MMT, Kattupalli −8%, Karaikal −4%. Mitigants are real — 100+ commodities, coast parity, 53% sticky cargo.Medium
ConcentrationMaterially improved, and under-appreciated. Mundra is now 38% of volume, 22% of revenue and 24% of EBITDA, against 62% / 39% / 40% in FY20. But capacity remains Gujarat-heavy (Mundra, Hazira and Dahej are 49% of domestic capacity), coal is still 32% of cargo, and Mundra alone is ~11.5% of all cargo handled in India. Customer concentration is genuinely low — the accounts confirm no single external customer exceeds 10% of revenue. The rising concentration is strategic: MSC is now JV partner at Mundra CT-3 and Ennore and is buying 49% of Vizhinjam.Medium
Regulatory & politicalThe moat and the risk are the same object. Mundra expires 16 Feb 2031 on a non-binding Letter of Comfort; the CT-3, CT-3 Extension and CT-4 sub-concessions await Government of Gujarat approval for final signing. The Indian Ports Act 2025 for the first time gives State Maritime Boards statutory power over non-major tariffs and mandates public tariff filing — no caps today, but the machinery now exists. Kerala says its consent was never sought for the $1.397bn Vizhinjam sale to MSC, and that sale is the funding cushion for the capex plan. The 2021 Mundra heroin case remains live, with ED raids as recently as June 2026 — APSEZ is not charged. The company's own risk register ranks Political (R1) and Regulatory (R2) above every commercial risk.High
Group & governanceDe-escalating but not resolved. Contagion is documented: APSEZ dollar bonds fell 3–5 cents on the Nov-2024 indictment and traded to 86–88 cents post-Hindenburg, while the operating business compounded EBITDA above 20%. The SEC settled in May 2026 for $18m with no admission and no director bars; DOJ charges against the three principals were dismissed with prejudice in August. But the presiding judge called the dismissal's irregularities “concerning”, rejected DOJ's claim that India had investigated, and denied DOJ's bid to drop the co-defendants' counts on 5 September. SEBI's Adicorp disposal turned on the LODR amendment being prospective, not on the substance of ₹1,282 Cr of round-trips. Separately OFAC settled with Adani Enterprises — not APSEZ — for $275m over Iran sanctions in May 2026.Medium-High
Balance sheetLeverage itself is comfortable — 1.9x reported, 2.4x lease-inclusive, against a 2.5x cap, with FFO/gross debt at 36% and no refinancing cliff. Two real exposures sit behind it. ~82% of debt was foreign-currency denominated against overwhelmingly rupee revenue; the hedge is structural rather than transactional, and a 1% INR/USD move shifts PBT by ₹120 Cr — so a 10% depreciation is roughly 8% of PBT. FY26 carried a ₹812 Cr net derivative loss against a ₹246 Cr gain the prior year. And capex discipline is the soft spot: ₹15,320 Cr spent against ₹11,000–12,000 Cr guided, with Vizhinjam Phase 2 (₹16,000 Cr) and a 2030 doubling still to fund.Medium

The cabotage item deserves particular attention. The 2018 relaxation allowing foreign-flagged vessels to carry domestic coastal legs was revoked on 21 January 2026 effective April, then deferred to at least October 2026 because the Hormuz crisis was pushing relay cargo through Indian ports. Vizhinjam is APSEZ's fastest-growing asset and a pure transhipment play. A hard reimposition sends that relay cargo back to Colombo, Singapore and Jebel Ali.
Two Bull-Case Planks That Do Not Survive the Data

1. “Private ports are taking share.” On official MoPSW data this stopped being true. Non-major growth ran +11.2% (FY24), +2.2% (FY25), +1.4% (FY26), while major ports accelerated to +7.0%. Non-major container throughput actually fell 0.19%. Gujarat, 64% of all non-major cargo, contracted 0.51%. APSEZ is still gaining share, but inside a shrinking base and increasingly via major-port PPP terminals — which is exactly what the Paradip and Deendayal wins are.

2. “Logistics reform will unlock huge savings.” The 13–14% of GDP figure underpinning most port-infrastructure theses has no Indian methodological basis. The NCAER–DPIIT study puts India's FY24 logistics cost at 7.97% of GDP — against a national target of ~8% by 2030. The efficiency dividend is largely already banked, and the incremental opportunity is far smaller than the story implies.

And a competitor being built by the grantor. Vadhavan — ₹76,220 Cr, 300 MMTPA, 20-metre draft, JNPA-led — is a west-coast container gateway aimed squarely at the trade Mundra serves, targeted from around 2030. It is a two-sided event: a terminal APSEZ can bid for, and a state-sponsored rival to its flagship franchise arriving at almost exactly the moment the Mundra concession comes up for renewal.
The thing that should most give an investor pause: the entire economic advantage — deep-draft berths, unregulated tariffs, and the land bank that feeds them — is a grant from state authorities that expires at Mundra on 16 February 2031. As of today all APSEZ holds is a non-binding Letter of Comfort whose price, in tariff-sharing and forced investment commitments, has not been named. And the listed template for what happens to a concession as the clock runs down is sitting in the same peer table: Gujarat Pipavav, best returns of the group, trading at 8.9x.

Recommendation, Support & Resistance

Stage 1 · Anticipation
ACCUMULATE ON WEAKNESS

The operating asset is genuinely good and the technical setup is the cleanest in this report series — all three timeframes aligned off a wave-(4) low, on a 151% volume surge. The legal overhang that suppressed the multiple for eighteen months has demonstrably lifted. But the entire sell-side is already positioned (23 Buy, 0 Sell, lowest target below the market), the stock is the most expensive listed port operator in the world at 19.3x EV/EBITDA, and two dated risks land within weeks. Anticipation here means a defined-size position with room to add, not a full weight at the top of a fifth wave.

Stage 2 · Confirmation
ADD ON THESE THREE
1. Mundra concessionA binding extension with named terms
2. CabotageOctober decision resolved without harm to relay
3. Domestic volumeRe-acceleration above 5%

The first one is the whole thesis. Every other risk here is manageable and most are improving — leverage is comfortable, ratings are rising, cash conversion is 89%, concentration is falling, and the legal chain has cleared. What cannot be managed is a concession that expires in February 2031 on a non-binding letter, with the sub-concessions for three Mundra container terminals still awaiting state approval for signature. Get a binding extension on disclosed terms and this is a high-quality infrastructure annuity at a full but defensible multiple. Don't, and the market will start doing to Mundra what it has already done to Gujarat Pipavav — which trades at 8.9x with better returns.
