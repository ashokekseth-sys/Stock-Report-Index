Company Overview

What They Do

Bharat Wire Ropes Ltd, incorporated in 1986 and acquired by the Mittal family in 2010 (listed on NSE/BSE in 2016), is one of India's largest manufacturers of steel wire ropes, specialty wires, strands and slings. Products span 6mm to 125mm diameter, up to 2,160 N/mm² tensile strength, and serve elevators, cranes, mining, oil & gas, ports/shipping, infrastructure, fishing, structural and defence applications.

Manufacturing capacity is ~72,000–78,000 MTPA across two Maharashtra plants — Chalisgaon (~66,000 MTPA, one of the world's larger single wire-rope facilities, ~₹500 Cr invested) and Atgaon — with backward integration into wire rods and German/South Korean machinery. The company holds BIS, RDSO, DGMS and ISO certifications, exports to 50+ countries, and counts India's defence establishment, Indian Railways, ONGC, Coal India, BHEL, major ports and private OEMs among domestic clients.

Sector placement: Materials → Steel → Specialty Wire Ropes — a narrow niche. The closest large listed peer is Usha Martin (significantly larger and more diversified); there is no exact pure-play comparator at similar scale, which limits the usefulness of any single peer multiple.

Sector Context

The Indian steel wire-rope market is estimated in the mid-hundreds of USD millions, with a mid-single to high-single-digit CAGR outlook, supported by infrastructure, mining, ports, elevators and energy capex. Global players — Bekaert, WireCo, Kiswire and Usha Martin — set quality and technology standards that Bharat Wire competes against on certification and price rather than scale.

Within this landscape, Bharat Wire is best framed as a credible domestic #2-type player focused on specialty and heavy-duty ropes — sizeable enough to serve defence, rail and PSU accounts at scale, but without the balance-sheet or global footprint of the sector's largest names.

Framing: A mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit sector growth rate is a modest tailwind, not a re-rating catalyst on its own — the stock's near-term path depends more on company-specific realisations and margin recovery than on sector beta.

The 17.36x FY20 gearing confirms genuine past financial distress (near-restructuring-level leverage); the CCPS conversion functioned as a de facto restructuring event, even though no formal "CDR" or explicit default/NPA terminology was found. No ICRA rating history was found, only CARE; an Aug 2025 CARE reaffirmation held ratings steady despite FY25 revenue coming in lower than envisaged and margin moderation from declining steel prices and higher logistics costs.
