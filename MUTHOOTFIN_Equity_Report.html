Company Overview

What They Do

Muthoot Finance is a Kochi-based non-banking finance company whose core product is the loan against gold jewellery. In plain English: a customer pledges gold ornaments, the company lends a fraction of their value (the loan-to-value, LTV), usually for a short tenor, and earns interest; if the customer does not repay, the company can auction the gold. Gold is the collateral and gold price is therefore the single most important external variable. The group also owns Belstar (microfinance), Muthoot Homefin (housing), Muthoot Money (gold loans; a merger into the parent is proposed with an appointed date of 1 Apr 2027, subject to approvals) and other smaller businesses.

FY26 consolidated revenue was ₹31,209 Cr (+54% YoY) and net profit ₹10,607 Cr (+98%); EPS rose from ₹132.84 to ₹263.79. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is ₹36,147 Cr and TTM EPS ₹303.86 (my arithmetic from the last four quarters, matching MarketSmith's ₹36,145.68 Cr sales). Consolidated loan AUM was ₹2,09,317 Cr at Sep-26 (+42% YoY), of which gold loans ₹1,93,212 Cr (+46%).

Key Competitive Advantage: Scale and brand in a collateral-backed niche: ">40% of NBFC gold loans" (Whalesbook, FY26), a large branch network (CRISIL: 7,541 branches, ~6,007 gold-loan; other counts conflict, see section 02) and a decade-long record of low credit cost. The flip side: the collateral is a volatile commodity, banks and new entrants are taking share, and Stage 3 rose sharply in Q2 FY27 (section 04).

What would lift it: non-gold above ~20% of AUM with stable asset quality (CRISIL's upgrade trigger mentions more diversified AUM and geography). South India is ~57% of branches and ~49% of gold AUM (CRISIL).

Regulation as a BarrierMixedRBI's harmonised gold-loan directions (effective 1 Apr 2026) apply equally to banks and NBFCs, narrowing the regulatory arbitrage that favoured NBFCs. Tiered LTV caps (up to ₹2.5 lakh = 85%, single secondary source; the draft had 75% flat and CRISIL called the final version a "breather"), a 12-month cap on consumption bullet loans and interest-first renewal rules.
Market ShareConflicting>40% of NBFC gold loans (Whalesbook) versus a much smaller share of all gold loans because banks hold far more (banks >₹12 lakh Cr vs NBFCs ₹3–3.5 lakh Cr — different bases, not merged). New entrants' gold loans grew 2.5x YoY to ₹61,900 Cr (Jun-26), 7.6% of retail gold loans. Manappuram has 4,054 branches and plans +500 gold branches in FY27.
What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

Brand and branches protect market share, not the collateral. They do not stop gold falling, they do not stop customers shifting to a bank, and they do not protect the unsecured subsidiary books (microfinance). Growth in FY26 and H1 FY27 also leaned on price: RBI-linked commentary (niftytrader) notes loan growth was driven more by gold price than tonnage, and the price/tonnage split for Muthoot is not disclosed in the sources found. Management said it traded some share for profitability.

Synthesis: The moat is real but narrower than the 9x P/E suggests it is priced for. The franchise is the strongest in its niche; the question is whether FY26's peak margins and low credit cost are normal or the top of a cycle. Regulation is levelling the field, banks are 55% of retail gold loans, and the first big stress datapoint (Stage 3 in Q2 FY27) has arrived.

Gold-Loan Defaults & Asset Quality — Priority Section

The newspaper article (added in v2): Times News Network (Times of India group), “Gold loan defaults see almost 2x rise in 5 mths”, Mumbai, citing the TransUnion CIBIL report Lending Unlocked: Gold Loans (Nov 2026). Sector-wide, and it names no lender, so it says nothing direct about Muthoot. Reported: loans overdue more than 90 days rose to 0.3% from 0.2% of a gold-loan book of about ₹22.5 lakh Cr; sanction value was up about 12% to ₹8.2 lakh Cr while the loan count fell about 15%; roughly 3.7 crore loans to 2.4 crore unique borrowers; a shift toward larger tickets; Gen Z borrowers up 19%. My arithmetic: “almost 2x” is a +0.1 percentage-point move on rounded figures, so the headline is large only because the base is tiny; the underlying rise could be anywhere from about 0.05 to 0.15 points. Why it still matters here: the larger-ticket shift is the same direction as Muthoot’s own plan (tickets above ₹50,000), which raises concentration risk, and it sits beside Muthoot’s Stage 3 jump (partly a definition effect, see below). Management's Explanation vs the Collateral Question

Management attributes the rise to customers adjusting to the new monthly/periodic interest-servicing norms (RBI directions effective 1 Apr 2026: interest must be paid before renewal or top-up, and bullet loans for consumption are capped at 12 months). That is a behavioural and rules-driven explanation, not a collateral-shortfall one, and it is management's attribution, not independently verified. Nuvama also flags asset quality "given the repayment-cycle change". The customer-level contagion rule explains why ~46% of Stage 3 is reclassified rather than freshly defaulted. If this reading is right the rise may be a one-time transition; if wrong, it is the first sign of stress.

Regulation & Supervision

RBI norms effective 1 Apr 2026: tiered LTV (up to ₹2.5 lakh = 85%; single secondary source, higher tiers unconfirmed), LTV measured on the amount repayable at maturity, renewal/top-up only after accrued interest is paid, and new disclosures of LTV, NPAs and auction recoveries. Auction-notice, reserve-price and surplus rules were not verified. RBI penalty: ₹5.80 lakh on 10 Jul 2026 for KYC/AML lapses (not gold-loan underwriting; company said no material impact). Earlier supervision: RBI cited weak LTV monitoring and opaque auctions in banks (bank gold-loan NPAs +62% to ₹2,445 Cr Jun-24 from ₹1,513 Cr Mar-24); Fitch and RBI's Jul-26 Financial Stability Report both flagged rising gold-loan risks, while a Business Today counter-view points to conservative LTVs and buffers.

My arithmetic — how much gold fall does it take? At the 65% overall LTV, collateral value must fall ~35% before the average loan is fully covered by nothing (1 − 0.65), before accrued interest; at the 72% Stage 3 LTV, ~28%. A 13–17% fall (India) therefore leaves a cushion for the average loan, but not for loans written at the 85% cap near the peak. If LGD stays ~8.8%, the loss on ₹7,020 Cr of Stage 3 would be ~₹618 Cr, about 19% of one quarter's PAT (illustrative; ignores interest income already accrued and provisions already held, which I did not find).
Two-sided read: Comfort — LTVs are conservative, LGD is low, ratings are AA+/Stable (CRISIL, ICRA) and the CRISIL downgrade triggers (Tier I CAR below 15%, non-gold deterioration) are far from met (standalone Tier I 20.27%). Concern — GNPA moved 3.87% from 2.28% in one quarter, Stage 2 also rose sharply, the 72% Stage 3 LTV is high, FY27 growth guidance was raised while asset quality worsened, and the ticket-size shift to ₹50,000+ loans (large-ticket concentration and current-versus-disbursal LTV not found) raises the stakes if gold stays weak.
