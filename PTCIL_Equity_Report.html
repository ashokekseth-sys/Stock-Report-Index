Company Overview

What They Do

PTC Industries Limited (originally “Precision Tools and Castings”, CIN L27109UP1963PLC002931) is a Lucknow-based investment-casting and metallurgy manufacturer, incorporated 20 March 1963. This is not a recent pivot into aerospace — the company has been in continuous investment-casting production since 1967, progressively climbing the alloy ladder from stainless steel to duplex/super-duplex, and now titanium and nickel-based superalloys for aerospace and defence. It licensed the Replicast® investment-casting process from UK's Castings Technology International in 1998 and later developed proprietary RapidCAST™ and ForgeCAST™ technologies.

The step-change came via its wholly-owned subsidiary Aerolloy Technologies Limited (incorporated 2020), which runs a titanium/superalloy melt facility at the new Strategic Materials Technology Complex in Lucknow (50 acres, UP Defence Industrial Corridor, ~₹1,000 Cr invested) — combined VAR+PAM+EB+VIM furnace capacity of ~6,000 tonnes/year. A second facility at Mehsana, Gujarat handles conventional investment-casting and is being expanded (+50,000 sq ft, automation/robotics). In Dec 2024 PTC also acquired 100% of UK's Trac Precision Solutions Ltd (Crewe — turbine blades, nozzle guide vanes, heat shields), bringing existing Rolls-Royce/Siemens/GE/Alstom/Metso/Emerson/Flowserve relationships into the group.

FY26 (consolidated) revenue was ₹603 Cr (+96% YoY), and the trailing twelve months stand at ₹697.43 Cr — the Q1 FY27 print alone (₹191.8 Cr, +~97% YoY per MarketSmith / +~83% YoY per one news source, both framed as approximate) shows the ramp continuing well past FY26's exit rate.

Key Competitive Advantage: Aerolloy's commissioned VAR furnace gives PTC a domestic titanium-melt capability that, per press coverage, few countries possess — paired with genuine, named relationships across Safran, Airbus (qualification-stage), Dassault, Honeywell and Blue Origin. The caveat that runs through this entire report: PTC is still a sub-₹700 Cr-revenue company being valued as if execution at global scale is already proven.

The global structural setup: China's share of global titanium metal supply rose from under 40% in 2018 to roughly 70–80% by 2024 (Project Blue data, via analyst Nils Backeberg — presented as a range, not a flat figure). Separately, Bain & Company estimates that engine shop-visit MRO capacity demand will exceed available supply by more than 17% by the end of this decade — this is an MRO-capacity statistic, distinct from titanium-metal supply, and the two should not be conflated even though both point the same direction. Project Blue also projects cumulative titanium demand exceeding 1.6 million tonnes by 2044 (not an annual figure), linked to roughly 46,000 new commercial aircraft over the next two decades.

Into this setup, PTC's wholly-owned subsidiary Aerolloy Technologies commissioned a German-built VAR-400 vacuum-arc-remelting furnace at the Strategic Materials Technology Complex, Lucknow, on 18 September 2025 (some social-media/press posts state May 2025 — that date is incorrect and is corrected here) — 1,500 tonnes/year capacity, ingots up to 1,000mm diameter and 10 tonnes. Press coverage frames this as placing India among roughly six nations (US, Russia, China, France, UK, India) able to melt aerospace-grade titanium — a framing sourced to PTC's own press materials, with no independent aerospace-industry-body confirmation found in this report's research.

What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

PTC's own execution risk — the VAR furnace was commissioned barely a year ago and is untested at scale. Capital-intensity/dilution risk from repeated equity raises. And the reality that, even domestically, a better-funded competitor (or MIDHANI itself, or a new entrant) could still contest this space given enough time and capital — the qualification barrier applies to everyone equally, not as a PTC-specific fortress.

Synthesis: The real, durable moat is the India defence-indigenization policy tailwind — not global technical superiority. Import dependency fell from 62% (FY02) to 12% (FY25); Positive Indigenisation Lists now explicitly cover titanium/superalloy aero-components; the FY2026-27 capital acquisition budget (~₹1.85 lakh Cr, 75% domestic-earmarked) creates captive demand regardless of whether PTC can out-scale Howmet or VSMPO globally. This is a policy-level moat that protects the opportunity, not a company-level moat that protects PTC's share of it.
