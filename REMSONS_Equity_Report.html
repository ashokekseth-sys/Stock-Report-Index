Company Overview

What They Do

Remsons Industries (incorporated 11 May 1971 as Remsons Cables, listed 1986) is a Mumbai-based, promoter-run auto-component manufacturer making control cables (Bowden cables), flexible shafts, gear-shift systems, push-pull cables and parking-brake mechanisms. It is a B2B Tier-1/2 supplier to OEMs — not an OEM itself — selling into 2-wheelers (Hero MotoCorp), passenger vehicles (Ford, plus exports), commercial vehicles (Ashok Leyland, Tata Motors) and farm/tractor equipment (Mahindra & Mahindra, a 50+ year relationship). The Kejriwal family has run the company since inception; current leadership is Krishna Kejriwal (CMD), Chand Kejriwal (WTD) and Rahul Kejriwal (WTD).

FY26 consolidated sales were ₹469 Cr (Screener) / ₹474–489 Cr (other sources), PAT ₹21 Cr (Screener) / ₹18.05 Cr (company press release, +25.6% YoY) — the two PAT figures conflict by ~15% and are both stated here rather than averaged. 7 plants (India + UK), 20+ OEM customers across 20+ countries (UK, Europe, North America, Brazil, Mexico, SAARC).

Key Competitive Advantage: Multi-decade OEM qualification relationships (50+ years with Mahindra; long tenure with Hero MotoCorp, Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Ford, JLR) inside an industry where APQP/PPAP validation cycles run 12–24 months — this is a switching-cost moat shared by every qualified Tier-1/2 supplier, not a Remsons-specific fortress (see §02). Recent order wins — a ₹300 Cr/7-yr Stellantis contract, a ₹80 Cr/10-yr Ford Turkey order and a ₹160 Cr/10-yr UK pedal-box order (production from late 2028) — show this qualification base is still converting into new business.

Claims of “strong in-house R&D” appear only in company-sourced/promotional material and are unverified. The Uni-Automation (sensors) and BEE Lighting (premium lighting) acquisitions are bought-in technology diversification, not organically developed IP.
What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

Losing the next platform RFQ to a better-capitalized, larger-scale incumbent (Suprajit) or a more diversified competitor (Minda Corporation) — the qualification barrier that protects existing programs applies equally to every qualified Tier-1/2 supplier bidding for the next one. It also does not protect against Remsons' own capital-allocation missteps (see the Astro Motors reversal, §16) or against the structural, multi-year EV shift away from mechanical gear-linkage cables specifically (see §16 / Sector).

Moat verdict: rests almost entirely on switching-cost dynamics inherent to the automotive qualification process — a dynamic every incumbent Tier-1/2 supplier enjoys, not a Remsons-specific advantage. On brand, cost and IP, Remsons is the smaller, less differentiated player relative to market leader Suprajit Engineering and diversified peer Minda Corporation. The company's edge is tenure and multi-segment OEM relationships, not structural pricing power or scale.

Synthesis — Two-SidedBull case: if the wave count is right, this is a genuine impulsive wave (iii) off a completed multi-month correction — a trend-reversal signal, not noise. A wave-(iv) pullback into the ₹118.04–123.09 pocket would be a textbook, tradeable correction ahead of a wave-(v) push toward the ₹144.48 (261.8%) target, still well below the user-cited but unverified ~₹235 2024 high. Bear case: a near-vertical, single-session move that overshoots the 200% extension with no consolidation is also the classic signature of a low-float overshoot or blow-off, not a clean wave 3 — and §03 found no confirmed fundamental trigger, thin float and near-zero institutional ownership behind this exact move. Levels that decide it: holding ₹114.81–118.04 keeps the constructive count alive; a decisive close back below ₹114.81 that keeps falling would invalidate it and open a retest of the ₹87.04 wave-(ii) low or the 52-week low near ₹75.23. Given the unresolved spike (§03), this report treats the technical structure as informative but not sufficient on its own to call the move real or sustainable.
