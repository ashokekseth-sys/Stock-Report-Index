Company Overview

What They Do

Hitachi Energy India builds the highways of electricity. It manufactures and executes HVDC converter stations (the technology that moves gigawatts of desert solar across a thousand kilometres), large power transformers, GIS/AIS substations, grid automation, traction transformers and power-quality systems — from 19 factories with 7,300+ employees. In plain English: when India needs to ship 6 GW from Khavda or Bhadla to its cities, only two consortiums on earth are trusted to build the terminals, and this company (with BHEL) is winning those orders.

FY26 revenue was ₹8,148 Cr (+27.6% YoY) with PAT of ₹988 Cr (+157%) — operating margin has tripled from ~5% (FY24) to 14.1% (16.4% operational EBITDA in Q4) as HVDC execution, services and exports kicked in. The order backlog stands at a record ₹29,555 Cr — 3.6x annual revenue of contracted visibility.

Key Competitive Advantage: The only listed Indian pure-play with parent-backed ±800 kV HVDC converter technology and a domestic execution record (Khavda–Nagpur, Bhadla–Fatehpur, Mumbai city-infeed) — a duopoly-like moat in the highest-value layer of India's ₹9.15-lakh-Cr transmission build-out, compounded by a ₹29,555 Cr backlog, the parent's global supply-shortage-driven export pull, and a large installed base feeding ~24% services orders.

A genuine migration up the value curve: HVDC went from nil (FY24) to ~13.5% of revenue and an estimated half of the backlog; exports rose from ~25% to 36.8% of orders; services run at ~24% of orders. The proof is the P&L — OPM 5.0% (FY24) → 14.1% (FY26, Q4 16.4%) and PAT margin ~3% → 12%.

Why not 5/5: HVDC is lumpy (two consortium mega-orders), the company discloses only a single segment (splits are inferred), and non-HVDC domestic orders were flat in FY26 — the base business hasn't re-rated, the project layer has.

Why The Selloff Overstates The Risk

The entrants don't play in the moat layer. TBEA, Nanjing Electric, New Northeast and Taikai compete in commodity transformers and AIS substations — not ±800 kV HVDC converter technology, where Hitachi Energy and Siemens form a near-duopoly and where India's marquee projects (Khavda–Nagpur, Bhadla–Fatehpur, Mumbai city-infeed) are already contracted.

₹29,555 Cr is already in the book. The backlog equals 3.6 years of revenue, contracted before any Chinese participation; 36.8% of new orders are exports and ~24% are services — both outside Indian government tenders entirely.

The street's second look agrees: Nomura (6 Jul) called the selloff an "overreaction"; Jefferies called the dip a buying opportunity (TP ₹43,145). The −10% day-one move priced a competitive threat that arrives, if at all, in future commodity-product tenders — not in the contracted HVDC pipeline.

Unchanged caveat: Chinese entry is a real long-term price-pressure risk in base products — and FY26's non-HVDC domestic order inflows were already flat. At 143x earnings there is no valuation cushion if that narrative builds.

Key Ratios — Industry-Contextualized

P/CFO  ·  Market Cap ÷ Cash Flow from Operations Elevated
~113x (₹1,41,182 Cr ÷ ₹1,245 Cr FY26 CFO)

Note: Cash generation is genuinely strong — CFO of ₹1,245 Cr (FY26) and ₹1,494 Cr (FY25) against PAT of ₹988/384 Cr means earnings over-convert to cash, helped by customer advances on HVDC contracts. But 113x cash flow is the steepest multiple in this report's history: quality Indian capital-goods franchises trade at 25–45x, and even the euphoric power-capex cohort sits at 50–90x. The multiple only normalises if FY27–28 delivers the consensus doubling of profit — this ratio is where the China-narrative risk bites hardest.

Trajectory: on FY25 CFO the multiple was ~95x; if FY27 CFO scales with consensus profit (~₹1,900–2,100 Cr), P/CFO lands at ~67–74x — still rich, which is the honest summary of this stock.

Inventory Turnover  ·  COGS ÷ Average Inventory Healthy
~4.0x (₹4,872 Cr COGS ÷ ₹1,205 Cr avg inventory)

Note: Average inventory = (926 + 1,485) ÷ 2 = ₹1,205 Cr; inventory days ~90 — excellent for long-cycle grid equipment (peers run 2.5–4x). The FY26 inventory build (+60%) is execution-driven: raw material staged against the ₹29,555 Cr backlog. The stronger working-capital story is receivables: down ₹493 Cr to ₹1,913 Cr even as revenue grew 28% (86 days → 60s), because HVDC mega-contracts come with customer advances. Net result: a capital-goods company running on net cash of ~₹4,600 Cr — the balance sheet is a fortress, whatever one thinks of the multiple.
