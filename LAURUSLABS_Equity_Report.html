Company Overview

What They Do

Laurus Labs is a Visakhapatnam-based pharmaceutical maker with three businesses: affordable medicines (generic APIs and finished formulations, historically antiretroviral (ARV) products for donor-funded tenders, now also oncology and other generics), a CDMO / custom-synthesis arm (small-molecule development and commercial manufacture for global pharma; the research notes call it the legacy “Laurus Synthesis” and no separate segment is reported), and Laurus Bio (precision fermentation, acquired as Richcore in Nov 2020). Newer options: a commercial-scale peptide block at Vizag (first block commissioned in Q2 FY27; over 16,000 L of solid-phase reactor volume), an oligonucleotide entry (Galenvs Sciences, Canada), a 49% KRKA oncology JV and a minority stake in the CAR-T company ImmunoACT. Management cites 125 active CDMO projects (a dated figure), more than 275 CDMO clients including 7 Big Pharma (Perplexity profile text) and 92 DMFs.

Q2 FY27 (announced 29 Oct 2026, consolidated): revenue ₹2,206 Cr (+33%), EBITDA ₹762 Cr at 34.5% (company; ₹749–750 Cr at ~34% on other definitions), PAT ₹441 Cr (+126%), EPS ₹8.17. H1 FY27 revenue ₹4,233 Cr (+31%), EBITDA ₹1,406 Cr (33.2%), PAT ₹809 Cr. FY26: sales ₹6,813 Cr (+23%), net profit ₹890 Cr, EPS ₹16.46 (₹16.47 on MarketSmith, rounding). Net debt ₹2,466 Cr (1.02x EBITDA) against gross borrowings of ₹2,625 Cr on Screener. CARE AA / A1+ Stable (reaffirmed 3 Jul 2026); India Ratings, CRISIL and ICRA ratings were not found (§16). Exports are the majority of sales, but the share is disputed: more than 80% on the Q2 call vs 55–60% in a Q1 summary — not reconciled.

Key Competitive Advantage: Integrated small-molecule chemistry from intermediates to API to formulation, a growing CDMO book (about 40% of revenue vs 21% in FY22, target above 50% by FY30), 92 DMFs and a clean audit run (59 regulator and partner audits in H1 FY27, none with critical findings, per the company) — not a patent moat. Margins moved because mix moved: the EBITDA margin is 10 points above a year ago. The edge is execution and customer stickiness in contract chemistry; the risks are lumpiness, one molecule worth ₹200 Cr or more a year, and capex that returns revenue only from FY28–29. Detail in §02.

What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

Lumpiness: one blockbuster molecule contributes ₹200 Cr or more a year (management declined to name it); CARE calls the CDMO revenue (about ₹2,080 Cr in FY26) lumpy. Donor funding and tenders: ARV demand rests on Global Fund, PEPFAR and in-country programmes; a January 2025 US funding pause hit ARV supply to programmes (DSIJ; Laurus impact not quantified). ARV share is unclear and sources conflict: a Q1 FY27 summary gives ARV ₹669 Cr (API ₹415 Cr, FDF ₹254 Cr), which is ~58% of that quarter’s ₹1,156 Cr affordable medicines (my arithmetic) although the same summary calls it “about one-third”; the research also cites ARV at 8–9% of mix and H1 FY26 segmentation of ~68% affordable medicines; the Q2 write-up splits ₹1,302 Cr into ₹625 Cr “API” and ₹677 Cr “formulations”. Q1 ARV revenue grew only 3.4% vs affordable medicines +10%. The 50% / 60% ARV-share claims in two third-party pages (one says “nearly 50% of business”, another “Global Fund/PEPFAR about 60% of volume”) contradict the company’s own mix and are not used here. Regulatory: a single bad inspection at a CDMO-critical site (Parawada, Atchutapuram) would hurt; the new peptide block has no FDA record yet. Working capital and capex: inventory days 317, capex ~₹3,000 Cr across FY27–28 (raised repeatedly: ₹1,000 Cr, then 1,500, then 2,000 at Q1 FY27), operating cash flow −26% in H1. Disclosure: no revenue guidance, no quantified customer concentration, no commercial / Phase III molecule counts, and management declines to comment on geopolitics or China restrictions. China+1 and BIOSECURE: the BIOSECURE Act was folded into the FY2026 NDAA and signed in Dec 2025 with grandfathering to about 2032 (Business Today); Macquarie sees the revenue effect lagging 12–24 months; management itself says RFP inflow is “not an impact of BIOSECURE” — so the tailwind is a market inference, not a Laurus-quantified one.
