Company Overview

What They Do

Azad Engineering forges and machines the parts that cannot fail inside a jet engine or a power-plant turbine — titanium and nickel-superalloy components (blades, vanes, discs) that spin at extreme temperature and stress inside aircraft engines, gas turbines and now India's own indigenous jet engine. In plain English: when GE, Rolls-Royce, Siemens Energy or DRDO need a rotating hot-section part machined to micron tolerances from an exotic alloy, Azad is one of a handful of qualified suppliers worldwide who can forge it, machine it, coat it and test it — all under one roof.

FY26 revenue was ₹603 Cr (+31.8% YoY) with PAT of ₹134 Cr (+54%) — EBITDA margin has climbed from 29% (FY23) to 37% on operating leverage as new dedicated OEM facilities ramp. The order book stands at ₹6,500+ Cr — roughly 11-12x FY26 revenue — spanning Energy, Aerospace & Defence and Oil & Gas.

Key Competitive Advantage: A rare combination of super-alloy (titanium/nickel) precision forging fused with in-house machining, coating and testing under one roof, backed by decades-long, part-by-part OEM qualifications across 45+ processes and ~1,700 approved parts — management estimates 18-20 years for a competitor to replicate the qualification base — spanning BOTH the aerospace/defence and energy-turbine end-markets simultaneously, where narrower peers like MTAR and Dynamatic compete in only one.

Product-Mix Shift Toward Higher-Value Segment
Rated 3 / 5 ■■■■■

The shift toward Aerospace & Defence is real but has stalled, not accelerated: A&D revenue share rose from 9.1% (FY23) to a peak of 17.7% (FY25), but dipped back to 17.2% in FY26 as new Energy-segment capacity (GE Vernova, MHI) came online and grew faster (+34%) than Aerospace (+25%).

The forward story is credible: management's own 5-year target is Energy settling at 55-60% of revenue with Aerospace/Defence and Oil & Gas making up the balance — a genuine diversification path — plus the DRDO turbojet delivery is a new, higher-prestige credibility marker. Withheld 2 points: FY26 actuals moved the wrong way, and margin gains so far look more like scale/operating-leverage than a pure mix effect (segment-level EBITDA isn't disclosed).

What Happened & Why It Matters Beyond The Headline

On 22 July 2026, Azad delivered India's first fully indigenous expendable turbojet engine (350 kg thrust class, designed by DRDO's Gas Turbine Research Establishment) to the Ministry of Defence — a genuine national self-reliance milestone for missiles, UAVs and drones, publicized by MoD the next day.

The commercial signal matters more than the symbolism: this is proof Azad can move from being a component-level forger to a systems-adjacent partner on a sovereign propulsion program — exactly the credibility upgrade that supports its stated ambition to deepen wallet share from 1-1.5% toward 10% across its OEM base.

The news landed alongside the stock's 52-week high of ₹2,530 (13 Jul) and preceded a Goldman Sachs target raise to ₹2,460 — the market read it as confirmation of the growth narrative, not just a one-off PR moment.

Caveat: management declined to disclose engine unit counts or delivery cadence on the concall ("it was years, then months, now weeks") — this is a credibility marker, not yet a disclosed revenue line.
