Company Overview

What They Do

TD Power Systems is a Bengaluru-based manufacturer of AC generators (2-pole and 4-pole, up to ~250 MVA) and a provider of turnkey power-plant solutions, supplying steam, gas, hydro and wind turbine OEMs and EPC contractors across an estimated 70–104 countries (figures vary by source). In plain English: when a global turbine maker needs the generator that sits at the other end of the shaft — converting the spin of steam, gas or water flow into electricity — TD Power is one of a handful of independent manufacturers, in India or globally, qualified to build it to spec.

FY26 revenue was ₹1,856 Cr (+45.2% YoY), with the gas-turbine/gas-engine generator segment the standout growth driver — explicitly management-attributed to AI/US data-centre power demand. Two orders totalling ₹142 Cr from “two major international gas engine OEMs” for grid-stabilisation units, base-load power and data-centre gensets landed in FY26, alongside “significant volume orders from a major US-based gas turbine OEM for data centre generators” — no source names the specific OEM customer(s), a genuine disclosure gap stated plainly rather than speculated on.

Key Competitive Advantage: On 13 Aug 2026 TD Power signed a 10-year Build-to-Print Manufacturing Framework Agreement with Siemens Energy, Inc. (extendable 24 months) to manufacture 2-pole generators to Siemens' own design/specs via individual POs — the stock jumped ~5% to a 52-week high on the news. This is the most credible moat leg in the business (see §02) but it simultaneously creates a new customer-concentration dependency of undisclosed size, and underscores that above 60MW/2-pole, TD Power's own technology is itself Siemens-licensed — the moat above that threshold is partly dependent on, not independent of, Siemens.

But it cuts both ways: it also creates a new customer-concentration risk (no disclosed % of revenue/capacity it could absorb). Cost AdvantagePlausible / UnverifiedIndia labour-cost arbitrage feeding a 79–93% export order mix is a plausible cost edge, but it is unverified with hard $/MW manufacturing-cost data versus Siemens Energy, GE Vernova, WEG or Chinese makers — stated as inferred, not evidenced.
Patents / Proprietary TechWeakNo TD Power-specific patents were found. The company's edge is characterised as in-house engineering know-how plus the new UK R&D design centre (up to 100MW+ capability) — not patent-fenced IP.
What This Moat Does NOT Protect Against

A capacity-ramp delay at Tumkur/Bengaluru or the new UK-designed platform. Chinese entrants (Dongfang, Harbin, Shanghai Electric) expanding share via bundled financing. Any softening of the AI/data-centre power-demand cycle the current order momentum leans on. And the fact that TD Power itself remains, above 60MW, a licensee of the very OEM (Siemens) it is now also manufacturing for — a dependency, not a fortress.

Overall verdict: a narrow, relationship/qualification-cycle-based moat, freshly reinforced by the Siemens Energy framework, not a wide moat — it does not support a “wide moat” narrative in a fundamentally spec-driven, competitive capital-goods segment. The switching-cost leg is real and current; every other leg is either weak, unverified, or a plain dependency on a larger partner.
